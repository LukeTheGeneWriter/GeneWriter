## 1. What shipped: the "fitness" rename

This closes the last open item of the 3-item metric-alignment audit (the
other two — Kmer's `fold_enrich` direction and CodonUsage's percentage
redesign — shipped in commits `6910019` and `6a5f215`).

### The problem

The scalar the GA selects on was called **fitness** everywhere, and
`change_vector.score_changevec()` in code. Both names pointed the wrong way:

- In a GA, "fitness" conventionally means *higher is better* and is what
  selection **maximizes**. This quantity is **minimized** — `kill_off()`
  weights its cull *toward* high scorers, `select_survivors()` uses it
  directly as a die-weight, `directed_evolution()`'s lookahead picks the
  synonymous alternative with the *lowest* value. Every reader coming to
  `ga.py` with GA background had to invert the word at every call site.
- It is also not a fitness in the biological sense. Nothing here measures
  reproductive success. It measures how far a candidate sits from a set of
  natural-genome baseline distributions.

### The rename

| was | now |
|---|---|
| `change_vector.score_changevec(vecs, weights)` | `change_vector.distance_from_optimal(vecs, weights)` |
| metric string `"fitness"` | metric string `"distance_from_optimal"` |
| `visualize.fitness_color_values()` | `visualize.metric_color_values()` |
| `change_vector.dist_from_optimal(codons)` | `change_vector.optimal_usage_score(codons)` |

That last row is a second, separate rename that the first one forced. There
was already a `dist_from_optimal()` in the same module — and it was itself a
misnomer: it returns the *optimal* score for a window (the best synonymous
codon frequency available at each position, averaged), **not** a distance.
Its one caller, `baseline.compute_codon_usage_analysis()`, is what subtracts
the window's actual score to get a distance. Renaming it to
`optimal_usage_score()` says what it does and frees the name for the
population-level metric, which really is a distance.

The persisted baseline field `CodonAnalysis.windowdistancesfromoptimal` was
**not** renamed — that would invalidate every existing `Standards/*.json`
for no gain.

### Compatibility

Hard rename, no alias. Both `ga._score_by_metric()` and
`visualize.metric_color_values()` special-case the literal string
`"fitness"` in their `ValueError` and name the rename with its date, because
saved schedules and pasted Colab configs predating today will still carry it.

Touched: `change_vector.py`, `ga.py`, `schedule.py`, `visualize.py`,
`baseline.py`, `scripts/colab_stress_test.py`, `Testing_GeneWriter.ipynb`,
and 5 test files. **482 tests passing** (`wsl -e python3 -m pytest tests/ -q`,
~75s). No behavior change of any kind — pure naming.

## 2. The math the GA actually runs on

Notation used throughout the rest of this document:

- `x = (c_1 … c_N)` — one candidate, N codons, translating to the fixed
  target protein. `d_i` is position i's degeneracy (number of synonyms).
- `v_t(x)` — term t's **change vector**, a length-N array of non-negative
  "how much does this position need fixing" scores.
- `T = {RareCodons, CodonUsage, CodonPairBias, GC, Kmer, Uracil}`.
- `w_t` — that term's weight (the `WEIGHTS` dict).

### 2.1 The two scalars, and the one structural property that matters

**Position score** (`ga._ranked_positions`) — drives mutation targeting:

```
p(x)[i] = SUM over t of  w_t * v_t(x)[i]
```

**Distance from optimal** (`change_vector.distance_from_optimal`) — drives
selection:

```
D(x) = SUM over t of  w_t * SUM over i of v_t(x)[i]
     = SUM over i of  p(x)[i]
```

Those are the *same quantity* under two different reductions. `D` is just
`p` summed over positions. That identity is the single most important
structural fact about this GA, and it is what makes the operators cohere:
the thing selection kills on decomposes exactly into the per-position signal
mutation aims at. **A position's contribution to `p` is exactly its
contribution to `D`.** Anything added later that breaks this identity (a
term that only produces a whole-sequence number, or a non-linear reduction
over positions) quietly decouples "where to mutate" from "what gets killed."

`D` is a **weighted L1 norm** of the stacked change vector — not an L2
magnitude. Worth being explicit about, since "magnitude of the change
vector" could mean either. L1 means N small deviations cost exactly as much
as one deviation N times bigger. If we ever want "one catastrophic position
is worse than many mediocre ones," that's an L2 (or L-infinity) reduction,
and it breaks the decomposition identity above — so it would have to be a
*separate* metric used for a specific operator, not a replacement for `D`.

### 2.2 The six terms

All six are "higher = worse." Their scales are **not** commensurable, which
is what `WEIGHTS` exists to fix (§2.3).

**RareCodons** — `_rare_codon_term`, window 15 codons.
Let `r_i` be 1 for a rare codon and 0 otherwise, and `s_j` the number of
rare codons in window j (0..15). The baseline stores how many natural
windows had each count, so `odds(s) = total_windows / count(s)`, i.e. the
**inverse empirical probability** of that window composition — `inf` for a
composition never observed. Window scores are smeared back onto positions by
`_windowed_average` (position i gets the mean of the windows overlapping
it), multiplied by a whole-sequence factor, and **gated to rare positions
only**:

```
v_RareCodons(x)[i] = r_i * windowavg_i( 1 / Phat(s_j) ) * z(overall_rare)^2
```

Note the `inf`: a never-observed window composition gives a genuinely
infinite score. `ga._finite_nonneg()` and `visualize._sanitize_for_color()`
both exist to absorb it. Far more common against a small or synthetic
baseline than against the real ~18,864-gene corpus.

**CodonUsage** — `_codon_usage_term`, **no windowing at all** (redesigned
2026-08-19). For each amino acid `a` present in x, with `n_a` occurrences:

```
pct(a,c) = count(a,c) / n_a           for each synonym c of a
Z_a      = SUM over c of  z( pct(a,c) )^2
                                       z-scored against the pooled natural
                                       per-gene percentage distribution
                                       for that exact (a, c) pair
v_CodonUsage(x)[i] = Z_{aa(i)}
```

Broadcast to every position using that amino acid — so the term's total is
`SUM over a of n_a * Z_a`, i.e. **occurrence-weighted**: an amino acid used
40 times contributes its deviation 40 times. Symmetric by design
(over-using a rare synonym and under-using a common one are both flagged).
Met and Trp contribute exactly 0 — their baseline is a single repeated value
and `distribution_fit.py`'s degenerate fallback returns z=0 always.

**CodonPairBias** — `_codon_pair_bias_term`, two layers, multiplicative
(redesigned 2026-08-19). With `dist_j = max(cpb_lit) − cpb_lit(c_j c_{j+1})`:

```
local(x)[i] = mean of the (at most two) pair distances touching position i
m(x)        = 1 + min( z(mean pair score vs cpbPerGene), 0 )^2   <- asymmetric
v_CodonPairBias(x)[i] = local(x)[i] * m(x)
```

The multiplier is deliberately one-sided: below-natural-mean CPB amplifies
local pressure; at or above the mean it is exactly `1.0` (neutral, not
zero — a merely typical sequence still passes its local signal through
unscaled).

**GC** — `_gc_term`, window 15 **nucleotides**, additive local/global split:

```
v_GC(x)[i] = mutable_i * ( localwindowz_i^2 + z(overall_GC vs gcPerGene)^2 )
```

`mutable_i` is 0 at positions where no synonym changes any base — no
mutation there can move GC, so there's no point flagging it. Local windows
are z-scored against a per-location-bucket baseline (`gc.windows[bucket]`,
bucket chosen by a majority vote over the window's F/T/I/S tags), smeared to
nucleotides, then averaged down to codons.

**Kmer** — `_kmer_term`, per k (the baseline holds k = 2..10), window 15:

```
score(w)  = 1 / (fold_enrich(w) + 0.1)        <- direction fixed 2026-08-19
overall_k = mean over that k's window scores
v_k[i]    = windowavg_i(score) * overall_k     (then averaged to codons)
v_Kmer[i] = SUM over k of v_k[i]
```

`fold_enrich = 0` (never observed in nature) gives 10.0, the worst score;
heavily over-represented tends toward 0. Note the `* overall_k` factor: this
is a *multiplicative* whole-sequence amplifier like CPB's, not an additive
one like GC's. The three terms with a global layer each do it differently.
That is deliberate per-term tuning, not an inconsistency to normalize away
without thinking about it first.

**Uracil** — `_uracil_term`. Count of a codon's bases that are `T` **and**
actually variable via some synonym, in 0..3. The one term with **no natural
baseline** — a flat "reduce U" objective, not a "match nature" one. Defaults
to weight 0.0. Its docstring carries a long, empirically verified warning: a
*negative* `weights['Uracil']` does **not** invert the objective, it breaks
it (`_finite_nonneg()` clamps negative totals to ~0, and lookahead would
then actively *grow* U content). Positive weight is the only correct
setting.

### 2.3 Weights

`WEIGHTS` is doing two jobs at once, and it's worth keeping them separate:

1. **Unit conversion.** The six terms have wildly different natural scales
   (an inverse probability that can be `inf`; a sum of squared z-scores; a
   raw lookup-table distance; a count in 0..3). Weights have to make them
   comparable before "sum them" means anything.
2. **Preference.** How much we actually care about each axis.

`weight_calibration.compute_intolerance_weights()` only does job 1, and does
it from data: weight proportional to `1 / CV`, where CV is the shape-aware
coefficient of variation of that term's own per-gene score across the
natural corpus (`value_at_zscore(±1)` half-widths around the fitted center,
not raw std/mean — the 2026-08-19 asymmetry fix). The logic: a term real
genes barely vary in is one nature is *intolerant* of perturbing, so
drifting off it should cost more. Normalized so calibrated terms average
1.0. Kmer (no per-gene array) and Uracil (no natural baseline at all) fall
back to whatever the caller passes.

**This does not touch job 2.** Any deliberate preference — "we care about
uracil more than CPB for this construct" — has to be layered on by hand, and
there is currently nowhere in the codebase that records the distinction: a
hand-set weight and a calibrated weight look identical in the dict. Worth
naming as a design gap before the next round of weight tuning.

### 2.4 The operators

**Growth / mutation** (`ga.replicate_and_mutate_random`,
`ga.directed_evolution`, `schedule._do_growth`). Each individual produces
`rate` offspring; each offspring is *either* random or directed, decided by
one Bernoulli draw per individual with probability `directed_fraction`
(default 0.5; the `directed_growth` step hardcodes 1.0).

- *Random*: every codon independently resampled uniformly from its synonym
  set with probability μ (default 0.05). The resample **includes the current
  codon**, so the real per-codon change probability is `μ(1 − 1/d_i)`.
- *Directed*: pick **exactly one** position, sampled with probability
  proportional to `p(x)[i]`, then greedily take the synonym minimizing `D`.
  With `lookahead=False`, take a uniformly random synonym instead.

So directed growth is **strict 1-opt hill climbing**, and random growth is a
large, uniform-ish jump. There is nothing in between. §5 is about that gap.

**kill_off** (`ga._kill_off_by_scores`). Removes `M = floor(T * percent_cut
/ 100)` units of *replicate mass* (`T` = total replicate count), one at a
time, each drawn from a categorical distribution over individuals with
probability proportional to `D_i`.

> **Non-obvious property, worth a deliberate decision:** the draw weight is
> `D_i`, the individual's score — **not** `number_i * D_i`. Per draw, a
> singleton and a 50-copy individual with the same `D` are equally likely to
> lose a unit. An individual therefore has to be hit `number_i` times to
> die, which means **replicate mass functions as a survival shield** rather
> than as proportional exposure. Whether that's the intended semantics is a
> real design question. It interacts directly with `flatten`, which destroys
> mass — an individual that has just been flattened is at its most
> vulnerable. If mass *should* be proportional exposure, the change is
> one line (weight by `number_i * D_i`); that would make `kill_off` a
> genuinely proportional cull and make flatten's trade-off cheaper.

**select_survivors** (`ga.select_survivors`). Cuts down to `target_size`
**distinct individuals** — a different currency from `kill_off`'s mass — via
Efraimidis–Spirakis weighted sampling without replacement: key
`= ln(u_i) / D_i`, remove the largest keys. Exact, O(n log k), and victims
are chosen with probability proportional to `D`. A *soft* top-N: a weak
individual can survive by chance and a strong one can occasionally be cut.
That stochasticity is the diversity-preservation mechanism.

**flatten** (`ga.flatten_generation`). Cashes replicate mass in for breadth:
each individual's round-start count becomes that many random draws from its
single-mutation neighborhood, repeated `recursion_limit` times, then every
survivor's count collapses to 1. Mass-conservative per round; the final
collapse is not. This is the existing **diversification** operator — it
fills a neighborhood in, uniformly, from wherever the individual already is.

**protect / kill_off_by_term / natural_range_cutoff.** Three different
escapes from the single-scalar regime, all added 2026-08-19.
`mark_protected()` and `protect_criteria` shield the top fraction by *any*
metric (union, not intersection), so an individual that is best-in-class on
one axis survives regardless of its aggregate. `kill_off_by_term()` culls on
one term's own score with no dilution from the others.
`kill_off_outside_natural_range()` is a hard per-axis guardrail: more than
`threshold` sigma off the natural per-gene distribution on *any* single axis
and the whole genotype dies, however good its aggregate looks.

### 2.5 Exact vs. diffed change vectors

Growth produces new genotypes with an **approximate** change vector, diffed
from the parent over the affected excerpt (`change_vector.diff_change_vector`)
rather than recomputed. `kill_off` / `select` / `flatten` each force a full
exact refresh first; `run_ga` does it every `refresh_every` generations.
That approximation is why the schedule DSL pays off — stack cheap growth
steps between precision checkpoints. The drift is bounded per step but
compounds, and nobody has measured how fast at real scale. If §5's
multi-position mutations land, note that a k-position change makes the diff
excerpt wider and the approximation correspondingly worse.

## 3. What kind of search this actually is

### 3.1 The geometry

For a **400-codon** protein at the standard table's mean degeneracy (3.05
synonyms per amino acid, unweighted over the 20 standard AAs — real proteins
differ by composition):

| quantity | size |
|---|---|
| whole sequence space | 3.05^400 ≈ **10^193.7** |
| single-mutant neighborhood, size of N₁ | **820** |
| double-mutant neighborhood, size of N₂ | **335,360** (10^5.5) |
| triple-mutant neighborhood, size of N₃ | **9.1 × 10^7** (10^8.0) |
| quadruple-mutant, size of N₄ | 10^10.3 |

`codon_tables.sequence_space_size()` computes the first row exactly for a
given protein, and `schedule._remaining_space()` already clamps population
sizes against it.

**N₂ is small enough to enumerate exhaustively per individual on the GPU.
N₃ is not — it has to be sampled.** That single fact shapes everything in §5.

### 3.2 Where random growth actually lands

At μ = 0.05, one random offspring differs from its parent at
`N * μ * (1 − 1/d) = 400 × 0.0336 ≈ 13.4` codons (sd 3.6 — essentially
always between 3 and 24). The radius-13 shell around a point contains
**~10^28** genotypes.

So random growth is not "exploring the neighboring basin." It is drawing one
sample out of 10^28, which is barely distinguishable from a fresh random
restart that happens to preserve a little of the parent. It contributes
diversity and almost nothing else — every bit of accumulated quality is
destroyed.

For reference, if we wanted random growth to make a genuinely *local* jump:

| `mutation_chance` | E[codons changed] |
|---|---|
| 0.05 (current default) | 13.4 |
| 0.01 | 2.7 |
| 0.005 | 1.3 |
| 0.0025 | 0.7 |

### 3.3 The gap

That leaves the search with two moves and nothing between them:

```
directed growth  ->  exactly 1 codon, greedily best      (pure exploitation)
flatten          ->  exactly 1 codon, uniformly random   (local diffusion)
random growth    ->  ~13 codons, uniformly random        (near-restart)
```

A basin of attraction under 1-opt hill climbing has some radius r in codon
Hamming distance. Moves of length 1 cannot leave it by definition once
you're at a 1-opt local optimum. Moves of length 13 leave it so thoroughly
that they land nowhere in particular. **The missing move is length 2–3: far
enough to leave the basin, near enough to keep everything the run has
accumulated.** That is exactly Luke's flagged item, and §5 is the design.

### 3.4 The multi-objective ceiling (this is the important one)

`D(x)` is a **linear scalarization** of a genuinely 6-objective problem.
That has a hard, well-known consequence:

> Minimizing a fixed-positive-weight linear combination can only ever find
> solutions on the **convex hull** of the Pareto front. Any Pareto-optimal
> sequence sitting in a *concave* region of the front is unreachable — not
> "hard to reach," but unreachable **for every possible choice of weights**.

Two practical readings:

1. **Weight tuning has a ceiling.** If the front is concave anywhere
   interesting, no amount of retuning `WEIGHTS` gets there. When weight
   tuning stops helping, that is the likely reason, and the fix is a
   different selection mechanism, not different numbers.
2. **The schedule DSL already contains two of the three classic escapes**,
   probably without that being the intent when they were built:
   - `kill_off_by_term` = **lexicographic / alternating pressure** —
     pressure one objective at a time, which *does* reach concave regions.
   - `kill_off_outside_natural_range` = **ε-constraint** — hard-bound each
     objective, optimize the rest.
   - The third, **Pareto-dominance ranking** (NSGA-II style non-dominated
     sorting plus crowding distance), does not exist in the codebase.

Given that `mark_protected()` already computes per-term top-fraction
rankings and takes their **union**, it is a small step from there to a
non-dominated front: "protect everything not dominated on all 6 axes" is the
same shape of computation. Worth costing out next session. The
crowding-distance half of NSGA-II is also the natural home for an explicit
diversity objective, which the run currently gets only as a side effect of
`select_survivors`' stochasticity and `flatten`.

## 4. FLAGGED: no gene can truly be optimal — measure the natural CDSs

**Luke's framing, verbatim:** *"In this multi-objective GA, no gene can truly
achieve optimal status. We'll want to calculate some 'distance from optimal'
metrics for the natural CDS as well to see what the acceptable levels are."*

This is right, and it now has a name in the code. `D(x) = 0` would require
every term simultaneously satisfied, and the terms **provably conflict** —
e.g. CodonPairBias's local layer wants the globally best-scoring adjacent
pair at every junction, which forces specific codons, which is exactly what
CodonUsage penalizes as an unnatural per-amino-acid percentage split. The
zero point is an unreachable corner (the "utopia point," in multi-objective
terms), not a target. Reporting `D` as though it were a score out of zero is
meaningless. `distance_from_optimal()`'s new docstring says so.

**The reference distribution we need is nature's own.** Concretely:

1. Score every natural CDS in the corpus with `distance_from_optimal()`
   using the same `WEIGHTS` and the same `AnalysisObjects` the GA run uses.
   Report the full distribution, not just the mean — these baselines are
   already known to be non-Gaussian (`distribution_fit.py` exists for
   exactly that reason), so quote percentiles.
2. **Normalize by length.** `D` is a sum over positions and therefore scales
   roughly linearly with N. Comparing a 200-codon gene's `D` to a
   2000-codon gene's is meaningless. Use `D/N`, or restrict the comparison
   to natural genes of similar length. Easy to get wrong, and it would
   silently invalidate everything downstream.
3. **Report per-term totals too, not just `D`.** The aggregate can hide one
   catastrophic axis behind five good ones — precisely the failure mode
   `kill_off_outside_natural_range()` was built to catch.
4. **The single most meaningful reference point is the natural CDS of the
   gene being optimized.** "Is our output at least better than the real
   gene?" is a claim we can make, defend, and put in front of a customer.
   Everything else is a percentile.
5. Then acceptance thresholds become statements like "at or below the 25th
   percentile of natural CDSs on `D/N`, and inside ±2 sigma on every
   individual term" — a *falsifiable spec* rather than a vibe.

**One caveat to state when we publish these numbers:** the baselines were
built from the same corpus, so each gene is partly scored against itself. At
~18,864 genes the self-contribution is ~1/18,864 — negligible, but say it
before someone else does.

### 4.1 A stronger version: some per-term optima are exactly computable

Worth an hour of the next session to check, because it would turn "distance
from optimal" from a purely relative number into a partly absolute one.
Several terms have exploitable structure:

- **CodonPairBias's local layer is a chain.** The sum of adjacent-pair
  distances depends only on `(c_j, c_{j+1})`, so the minimum over all
  synonym choices is a textbook Viterbi / shortest-path DP: **O(N·d²) ≈
  3,700 relaxations** for a 400-mer. Trivially fast. (The global multiplier
  depends on the mean pair score, so an exact joint optimum needs one outer
  pass over candidate mean values — still cheap.)
- **Uracil is position-separable.** Minimum is immediate.
- **CodonUsage is per-amino-acid separable** — for each `a`, choose the
  integer allocation of `n_a` occurrences across its synonyms minimizing the
  sum of squared z-scores. Small independent integer programs, exactly
  solvable.
- **Kmer (k ≤ 10) needs at most 4 codons of context** → `d^4 ≈ 87` DP states
  per position, ~35k total. Feasible.
- **RareCodons' 15-codon window** needs the full binary history →
  `2^15 = 32,768` states per position, ~1.3 × 10^7 total. Feasible, just
  bigger.

So a per-term utopia value looks computable for essentially every term. What
**blocks a single joint DP** over all terms is CodonUsage: it depends on
global per-amino-acid *counts*, not on local context, so it doesn't fit the
chain. That's the specific obstruction to naming an exact global optimum —
worth stating precisely rather than assuming the whole thing is intractable.

Even without the joint optimum, having each term's own minimum gives a real
**utopia point** and therefore a meaningful normalization: report each term
as `(actual − optimum) / (natural median − optimum)`, i.e. "how far from
optimal, in units of how far nature typically is." That is a number a
non-specialist can read.

## 5. FLAGGED: double and triple mutations as basin escape

**Luke's framing:** double/triple mutations as a way to *forcibly eject
sequences from a safe neighborhood and colonize new local optima*.

### 5.1 Why this is the right move, quantitatively

From §3.2–3.3: at a 1-opt local optimum, every one of the ~820
single-mutant neighbors is worse by definition. The run has exactly one way
out today — random growth — and that's a 13-codon jump into a
10^28-genotype shell, which discards everything the individual has
accumulated. Multi-position directed mutation is the missing operator, and
the neighborhood sizes make the design almost decide itself:

- **k=2: 335,360 neighbors.** Exhaustively enumerable and scorable per
  individual on the GPU — `gpu_change_vector.batch_calculate_change_vectors()`
  already handles populations of this order (that's what
  `suggest_chunk_size()` is for). We can compute the *exact* best double
  mutation, not sample it. That's a genuinely strong operator: it finds
  escapes no sequence of single steps can reach.
- **k=3: 9.1 × 10^7 neighbors.** Not enumerable per individual per
  generation. Has to be sampled — and sampled *smartly*, which is where
  `p(x)[i]` comes in: draw the k positions with probability proportional to
  `p(x)[i]` (the same distribution `directed_evolution` already uses), i.e.
  mutate the k positions the change vector is most unhappy about,
  simultaneously.

### 5.2 Why simultaneity is the whole point

This is not the same as two directed steps in a row. Two sequential 1-opt
steps require the *intermediate* to be accepted, and at a local optimum it
won't be. A simultaneous double mutation can cross a barrier where each
individual step is uphill but the pair is downhill. That is precisely the
class of move a 1-opt hill climber provably cannot make — the reason this is
worth building at all, rather than just running more generations.

The biological analogy is exact and worth keeping in the docstring: this is
compensatory mutation / epistasis. Two changes that are each deleterious
alone but beneficial together.

### 5.3 Sketch

A new `@register_step("saltation")` (or `"k_opt_burst"`) schedule step:

```
{"kind": "saltation",
 "k": 2,                     # 2 = exhaustive, 3+ = sampled
 "targets": {...},           # which individuals -- see §6
 "samples": 5000,            # ignored when k=2 and exhaustive is affordable
 "position_weighting": "p",  # proportional to p(x)[i], or "uniform",
                             # or "conflict" (§6.4)
 "accept": "all"}            # colonize, don't filter -- see below
```

Design points that need a deliberate call, not a default:

- **Accept unconditionally.** If we only keep improving double-mutants,
  we've built a better hill climber and we still never leave the basin. The
  point is *colonization*: emit the offspring into the population and let
  `kill_off` / `select_survivors` decide over the next few generations. That
  implies pairing saltation with a **grace period** — a `protect` step on
  the new lineage for a generation or two, or selection kills every colonist
  before it can descend into its new basin. The `protected` flag and the
  `protect` step from 2026-08-19 are exactly the right mechanism; this is a
  strong second use for them.
- **Exact vectors, not diffed.** A k-position change makes
  `diff_change_vector`'s excerpt wider and its approximation worse, and
  these offspring are precisely the ones that need accurate scores (they
  will look bad initially by construction). Batch them through
  `batch_calculate_change_vectors()` the way `_flatten_round` already does
  for brand-new neighbors.
- **Don't mutate through the special-translation sites.** Selenocysteine
  UGA/SECIS, non-AUG starts, frameshift junctions
  (`special_translation.py`). Already open backlog item 3, and a k-position
  burst is *more* likely to hit one than a single directed step. If
  saltation ships first, it needs at minimum a position-blocklist parameter.

### 5.4 Relationship to `flatten`

Complementary, not redundant, and the distinction is worth keeping straight:

| | `flatten` | saltation |
|---|---|---|
| move length | 1 codon | 2–3 codons |
| direction | uniformly random | proportional to `p(x)[i]` (or conflict-weighted) |
| cost | replicate mass | new offspring |
| effect | **fills in** the current neighborhood | **leaves** the current neighborhood |

Luke's own framing: *"We already have flatten which should fill in
neighborhoods with a lot of diversity by cashing in mass. This new
edge-focused mobility would also let us explore in new ways."* Exactly —
flatten is intensification by diffusion, saltation is escape.

## 6. FLAGGED: edge-focused targeting — who gets ejected

**Luke's framing:** use the magnitude of the change vector to *selectively
target sequences on the edges of safe neighborhoods, so they can achieve
maximal mobility out into new territory instead of starting from the center
of a safe neighborhood.*

This is the sharpest of the three ideas, and the one that needs a definition
before it can be built. "Edge of a safe neighborhood" has at least four
reasonable formalizations, and they do not agree with each other.

Formally, the "safe neighborhood" is the sublevel set of sequences with
`D(x) ≤ θ`, and its connected components under 1-opt moves are the basins.
"On the edge" then means one of:

**6.1 Scalar band — `D(x)` near θ.**
The worst survivors: highest `D` among individuals that made the last cut.
Free (already computed), and it is the literal reading of "use the magnitude
of the change vector." But it conflates *bad* with *peripheral* — a
uniformly mediocre individual sitting dead centre in a shallow basin scores
the same as a genuinely marginal one. Cheap prefilter, weak on its own.

**6.2 Concentration of `p(x)` — the good cheap one.**
Look at the *shape* of the per-position vector, not just its sum. Let
`q_i = p(x)[i] / D(x)` and take the entropy `H(x) = −SUM q_i ln q_i` (or a
Gini coefficient — same idea):

- **Low H** = one dominant hotspot. This individual is *pinned by a single
  bad position*, which means one directed mutation probably fixes it. It is
  heading for the centre of its basin. Leave it alone.
- **High H** = dissatisfaction spread thin across many positions. No single
  mutation helps meaningfully. **This is a stuck individual — the edge.**

Free (`p(x)` is already computed for every individual by
`_ranked_positions`), and it directly distinguishes "centre-seeking" from
"stuck," which §6.1 cannot. **Recommended as the primary criterion.**

**6.3 Lookahead gap — the accurate expensive one.**
`Delta(x) = D(x) − min over y in N₁(x) of D(y)`. `Delta ≈ 0` means no single
mutation improves anything: a **certified 1-opt local optimum**, which is
the exact, unambiguous definition of "has nowhere left to go." This is the
ground-truth criterion. It costs a full 820-neighbour scan per individual —
but `directed_evolution_batch()` already does most of that work when
`lookahead=True`, so the marginal cost may be much lower than it looks.
**Recommended usage: 6.2 as a cheap prefilter over the whole population, 6.3
as a confirming check on the shortlist.**

**6.4 Term conflict — the multi-objective definition, and the most
interesting one.**
The other three all reduce the multi-objective problem to a scalar before
asking the question. But in a genuinely multi-objective search, "edge of the
safe neighborhood" should mean *on the Pareto front*: improving any one term
now costs another. A concrete measurable form — for the top positions of
each term's own `v_t`, check whether two terms both flag the same position
while preferring *different* synonyms there. High overlap-with-disagreement
means this individual is trapped by a genuine trade-off, not by a fixable
defect. That is the truest reading of "edge," and it connects straight back
to §3.4's convex-hull ceiling: these are exactly the individuals a linear
scalarization can never help.

More expensive, and it needs design work, but it's the version that would
actually let the run colonize the concave parts of the front. Worth scoping
even if 6.2 ships first.

### 6.5 Why "edge, not centre" is the right instinct

Worth spelling out, because it's the load-bearing argument: an individual at
the centre of a basin has to spend its multi-mutation budget just getting
back to where it already is — it is surrounded by *its own* basin in every
direction, so a k-step burst most likely lands it back inside. An individual
at the edge is already adjacent to unexplored territory, so the same
k-mutation budget carries it much further in useful terms. Same operator,
same cost, far better expected yield. Targeting matters at least as much as
the operator itself.

### 6.6 One caution

Don't let edge-targeting become "always mutate the worst individuals." That
is just a slower `kill_off`, and it burns the operator on individuals that
are about to die anyway. The criteria above should be applied **within the
surviving, high-quality population** — the individuals worth *keeping* that
have stopped improving. The natural schedule placement is right after a
`select` step (survivors only) and, per §5.3, paired with a `protect` grace
period for the colonists.
